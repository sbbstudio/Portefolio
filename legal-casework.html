Two organisations.
One register.
Every claim traced.

Two community associations were opposing a quarry expansion in western Norway. Neither had counsel. Both needed correspondence a regulator would have to answer in writing — and it has since answered. The associations acted in their own name throughout; I built and ran the system behind their correspondence.

I built the document control first and the arguments on top of it. Every factual assertion in every letter resolves to a numbered record with a verifiable source — or it was not written. The register held 582 documents by the time the letters went out.

The drafting was done by AI agents I built and directed — Claude-based, each with a defined role and a source gate it cannot argue with. That is why the controls on this page exist: the system is held to the standard you would hold a junior lawyer to, in writing. Nothing went out until I released it — and the register is what makes that supervision honest: any claim in an outgoing document traces to its source without re-reading an archive.

A claim can never travel above its tier

A document does not exist
until it is in the register.

One register is the source of truth. Everything downstream — findings, authority, drafts — points back to it and cannot outrank it.

DocumentedA verifiable source exists. Only these carry a hard claim.
To be verifiedExistence uncertain. Cannot be cited.
Registered without documentKnown to exist, not held. The absence is a record.
MissingNamed, requested, not obtained. Counted, not hidden.
Grade is a ceilingEvery claim carries a grade — documented, secondary, assessment, open question — and no claim may be presented above it.
Verbatim or nothingA finding requires an exact quotation, cross-checked against an independent source, before it can be used.
Statutes are version-controlledA provision is tested as it stood on the date of the decision — a citation that predates its own law fails.

The register is the argument. The letters are only where it surfaces.

Any system that answers legal questions from a controlled corpus faces the same three questions: does the source exist, does the quotation match it exactly, was the provision in force at the relevant date. Answer those mechanically and the rest of the argument can be trusted.
